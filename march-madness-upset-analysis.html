March Madness upsets: the full analysis

We counted all 2,520 men's tournament games from 1985 to 2025. First-round upsets are only the start. The second round is wild too, low seeds almost never survive the last weekend, and the shape of March barely changes.

The second round is not safe either

Winning once buys a low seed a harder game, usually against a top seed.

The 11 seed is the best bet to win twice: 27 of its 62 winners did.

The 9 seed is the trap: it wins the coin flip, then meets a 1 seed. Only 8 of 83 won again.

Small print on the 15s: only 11 have ever won a first game, and 4 of those 11 reached the Sweet 16. One, Saint Peter's in 2022, went to the Elite Eight.

Patterns that keep repeating

  • The 11 seed keeps getting better. Its first-round win rate by era:
  • A Cinderella almost always shows up. A double-digit seed reached the Sweet 16 in 38 of 40 tournaments, the last 17 in a row. A normal year has about two of them.
  • Somebody's 5 seed goes home early. At least one 12 beat a 5 in 34 of 40 years. At least one 10 won in 37 of 40, and at least one 9 in 37 of 40.
  • The last weekend still belongs to the top. One seeds have won 26 of 40 titles. A Final Four of all four 1 seeds has happened only twice, in 2008 and 2025.
  • No seed line is owed anything. Six 11 seeds have reached the Final Four. Zero 12, 13, 14, 15, or 16 seeds ever have.

And here is a typical first weekend, start to finish:

  • About 8 upsets on Thursday and Friday, led by the 9, 10, and 11 lines.
  • About 4 or 5 more on the weekend, as low seeds win second games.
  • About 2 double-digit seeds in the Sweet 16, next to about 10 teams seeded 4 or better.
  • One or two 1 seeds in the Final Four (the average is 1.65), and a champion who is most likely a 1 seed, as 26 of 40 have been.

These are history's averages, not predictions about any 2027 team.

How this differs from the ratings sites

KenPom, Torvik, and the other ratings sites rate teams, and they are good at it. This page borrows one of their numbers and names it every time.

What none of them publishes is the tournament itself, counted: how many upsets a normal year holds, which seed lines bleed, what a typical first weekend looks like.

And the tools that fill in a bracket for you, in 30 seconds, for a price? That is the one thing this site will never do. The counts are on the table. The calls stay yours.

Common questions

What does history say will happen in March 2027?

History cannot name teams, but the shape is steady: about 8 first-round upsets (the middle half of years land between 7 and 10), about 4 or 5 more upsets in the second round, and about 2 double-digit seeds in the Sweet 16. The 2027 field is not set until Selection Sunday, March 14, 2027.

What is the deepest run a low seed has ever made?

A 15 seed, Saint Peter's in 2022, reached the Elite Eight. Two 12 seeds have reached the Elite Eight. Six 11 seeds have reached the Final Four. The lowest seed ever to win the title is an 8 seed, Villanova in 1985.

Do these numbers include the First Four or the Opening Round?

No. Every rate here counts the main bracket only, starting at the round of 64. In 2027, 12 Opening Round games come first, and the winners join the same 64-team bracket.
