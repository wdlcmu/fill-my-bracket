What is March Madness?

March Madness is the NCAA college basketball tournament. Teams play single-elimination games until one champion is left. Lose once, and your season is over.

One loss, and you are out

The tournament crowns the national champion.

It is played every spring, mostly in March.

In 2027, 76 teams enter each tournament.

There is a men's tournament and a women's tournament.

The field is split into four regions.

Each team gets a seed, from 1 to 16.

Winners move on. Losers go home.

After the Opening Round, 64 teams remain.

Then come the First Round and the Second Round.

The Sweet 16, the Elite Eight, and the Final Four.

Where the name came from

The phrase started in Illinois high school basketball.

An official named Henry V. Porter used it in 1939.

The first NCAA tournament was also played in 1939.

CBS broadcaster Brent Musburger used the phrase on air in 1982.

He meant the NCAA tournament, and the name stuck.

Why everyone fills out a bracket

Before the games start, fans predict every winner.

That prediction sheet is the bracket.

Offices, schools, and families run pools on it.

You do not need to follow basketball to play.

Plenty of brackets are picked by mascot or by colors.

Common questions

What is March Madness?

March Madness is the NCAA Division I college basketball tournament. In 2027, 76 teams enter each tournament, men's and women's. Every game is single-elimination: lose once, and you go home. The last team standing is the national champion.

Why is it called March Madness?

An Illinois high school official, Henry V. Porter, used the phrase for basketball in 1939. CBS broadcaster Brent Musburger used it for the NCAA tournament in 1982, and the name stuck. Most of the games are played in March.
