How to pick upsets in March Madness

Pick a few, in the right spots. About one first-round game in three is an upset, and most of them live in the same few matchups.

Five rules, from the seed math

  1. Start with the 12-over-5 game. It hits about 35 times in 100.
  2. Add an 11 seed over a 6 seed. About 38 in 100, since 1985.
  3. Take two or three first-round upsets. That matches history without betting the bracket.
  4. Stop before the late rounds. Favorites rule late. Keep your champion a 1 or 2 seed.
  5. Never spend a pick on 16 over 1. Twice, ever, in more than 160 tries.

Treat the 8-vs-9 game as a coin flip, not an upset.

One low seed in your Sweet 16 is normal. Two is a story.

Common questions

How many upsets should I pick in my bracket?

Two or three in the first round is the normal range. Since 1985, about one first-round game in three is an upset, but they cluster in a few matchups, like the 12 seed against the 5 seed.

Should I pick a 16 seed to beat a 1 seed?

History says no. A 16 seed has beaten a 1 seed twice in more than 160 tries: UMBC in 2018 and Fairleigh Dickinson in 2023. It is a fun pick that almost always costs you a game.
